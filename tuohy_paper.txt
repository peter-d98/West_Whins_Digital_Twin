ABSTRACT  
The objective of the ‘Orchestration of Renewable Integrated Generation in Neighborhoods’ ( ORIGIN) project 
was to maximize use of local renewables and minimize energy imports. This paper presents the ORIGIN 
approach, demonstration sites, and key findings with a focus on load shift mechanisms and results. Focus is on 
a 75 building predominantly  residential community with wind and solar generation . The ORIGIN system  used 
forecasts of weather, renewable generation , demand, and load shift opportunities, as inputs to  optimization 
algorithms which output information to displays  and remotely controlled loads. Web and phone widgets 
provided current and forecast green energy availability. Information widgets alone did not give  a measurable 
demand response. A financial incentive (green energy tariff) was added, an initial response was seen in 47% of 
households, 21% continued to  the end of  the 6 month tariff trial, p rojected across t he c ommunity this 
response was projected to give a 6MWh (1 .2%) reduction in grid imports from 511 to 505MWh p.a.  A remote 
control approach  was developed applicable to appliances,  EV charging, water pumping, mobile device 
charging, and space and water heating . It was implemented for existing solar thermal systems with electric 
backup, this gave a 44% reduction in summer electricity use, and 22% increase in loads met from renewable 
electricity. This scheme was modeled to give a 13% reduction in  community electricity imports from 511 to 
446MWh p.a. Future app lication of ORIGIN type systems, and methods for their design and integration are 
discussed.

1.  INTRODUCTION  
There have been many efforts worldwide focused on load shaping and demand side management through real 
time pricing, storage and control schemes  [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. The ORIGIN project [10, 11, 12] took three 
existing eco -communities with high local renewable integration and sought to develop advanced algorithms 
and methodologies that could significantly increase the extent to which use is made of the local renewable 
generation and reduce the extent to which energy needs to be imported to the community.  
The ORIGIN hypothesis was that a system could be developed to make better use of local renewables through : 
(i) stimu lating occupants to change behaviors , and (ii) remote control of loads . This concept wa s tested 
through developing, deploying and evaluating such a system. 
This paper is intended to give an overview of the ORIGIN  project and summarize a selection of key outcomes 
focused on load shift mechanisms and observed results. Firstly the demonstration site is described. Then  the 
overall ORIGIN methodology s pecified. Next the information  feedback and variable tariff system s and results 

are described. The remote control approach is then described and illustrated for  a solar thermal system for 
space heating and hot water. Results and wider implications are discussed including how buildings and systems 
can be designed to have increased energy autonomy and reduced grid dependence. 
This paper cove rs the broad scope of the ORIGIN  project and necessarily gives only selected highlights. The 
development of the user interfaces  and mathematical algorithms are described in more detail elsewhere [13, 
14, 15, 16].  
2.  DEMONSTRATION SITE . 
The ORIGIN project engaged with three eco -communities with high renewable generation: Findhorn in the 
north of Scotland, Damanhur in the north of Italy and Tamera in central Portugal. These sites have very 
different characteristics : Findhorn has 4 wind turbines with 750kW wind capacity, a grid connected private 
wire electrical network, over 75 buildings including 15 non -domestic buildings, high penetration of solar 
thermal systems, a range of heating systems including heat pumps, stand alone and district biomass, generally 
well insulated buildings including some approaching Passivhaus  standard, some photovoltaic generation, and 
some electric Vehicles; Damanhur has high levels of public grid connected photovoltaic  panels, biomass and 
solar thermal systems serving a large commercial and retail building plus a number of 20 person communal 
residences; Tamera has a large islanded photovoltaic and battery storage system, a sma ll number of shared 
community buildings, solar thermal systems, electric vehicles and water pumping loads. Given its larger scale, 
the fact that its systems form a super -set of those in the other  communities, and it being typical of future 
northern EU settlements, the focus in this paper is on Findhorn [17]. 
The 750kW of wind generation at Findhorn allows the community to be around 'net zero' in terms of its 
electricity supply from the grid but the actual supply / demand balance is highly variable. Figure 1  shows the 
renewable supply to demand matching for 4 days in February 2015 illustrating periods when surplus is 
exported to the grid and shortfall when grid electricity is imported. The situation during the year from 1st 
September 2014 to  31st August 2015  was that electricity consumed was 1132MWh and local renewable 
generation was 1205MWh but only 621MWh of the local generation was consumed and the rest (584MWh) 
exported to the grid, imports from the grid were 511MWh . The aim of ORIGIN was to re -shape the l oad to 
make more use of the local renewable generation and reduce energy imports.

3. THE ORCHESTRATION SYSTEM  AND ALGORITHMS . 


The ORIGIN system was cloud based, inputs were received from monitoring and/or control devices, algorithms 
used this data to generate information  and control outputs which are delivered back to the communities  and 
individual buildings. The informational feedback was through a library of widgets viewed through web portals 
and displayed on personal computer, tablet, or phone. Direct control was achieved through remote switching 
via 'smart plugs' either directly powering loads or powering relays inserted into existing control circuits.   
To drive performance improvement a set of algorithms was developed to deliver  load shaping to best match 
with forecast future generation. The ORIGIN algorithm component parts are: (i) a weather prediction algorithm 
(WPA), (ii) a renewable generation prediction algorithm (RPA), (iii) a demand prediction algorithm  (DPA), (iv) a 
generation/demand gap analysis , (v) a load shift opportunity prediction  algorithm (OPA), and (vi) an 
orchestration and control algorithm (OCA) (Figure 4).   
The OPA is used to produce predictions of future loads and a set of vectors representing possible load shifts 
associated with those future loa ds capturing: timings, shifted load size, and cost factors including efficiency 
and energy penalties associated with increased losses , reduced renewable heat inputs,  or reduced efficiencies 
etc. The OCA at each iteration sifts these opportunities and selec ts the optimum set of control actions to best 
match the required profile within the optimization criteria , and sets up a  control list to feed information 
displays and remote controls to shape the future load.   
The time-step between iterations of the algor ithm is flexible but currently set to 30 minutes although different 
timebases are used to inform different aspects e.g. tariff commitments are triggered once per day and remain 
fixed for a 30  hour horizon  to allow residents to plan their energy consumption .  The ORIGIN system utilizes 
weather, demand and generation forecasts based on mathematical and statistical methods [13, 14, 15]. 
 
Fig. 4. The ORIGIN algorithm component parts. 
The approach taken was to implement the demand response mechanisms in a staged way in order to allow the 
impacts of each to be distinguish ed. Initially informational feedbacks were provided at Community level, later 
personalized feedback information at household level was provided, a differential tariff mechanism was added 
next, and finally the remote control actuations were implemented  in a range of pilot studies . These various 
implementations are described in the following sections, results presented  and then discussed, and future 
work proposed.

Some motivated participants were observed to have made a large effort to use appliances in low tariff periods

participants’ tended to be people with energy use patterns that did not lend themselves to engagement or 
who did not choose to make it a priority. Responses were less in un -occupied or sleeping times even for 
motivated participants, as would be expected.

5. THE REMOTE CONTROL SYSTEM . 
Load switching hardware based on remote control of smartplugs was implemented into existing systems to 
give remote control of thermostatically controlled loads (TCLs) and electrically controlled loads (ECLs).  
The load shift opportunity predic tion algorithm (OPA) produces a set of multi -parameter vectors capturing 
forecast potential for advancing or delaying the  loads within the  allowed service window.  The optimization 
algorithm then selects appropriate control actions and implements these through the smart plugs.   
The electrically controlled loa ds (ECLs) are simply controlled to allow shifting of the predicted loads within 
negotiated service windows e.g. service must be complete by m orning, must be complete by 5pm etc.  
For space and water heating TCLs the service window is more complex and model based predictive control was 
developed to a llow the characteristics of systems, losses and de -stratification, user comfort, user behavior , 
and weather effects to be incorporated. The remote control algorithm for TCLs is described below, ECL control 
is a subset.. 
For space and water heating the smart plugs are implemented to override the standard on/off control signals, 
thermostats (and safety) are not affected and users can resume normal operation if desired.  
At Findhorn figure 11(a) and (b) show the total daily profile of heating and hot water load for the electrically 
heated dwellings  (14 Centini dwellings, and 25 Whin  dwellings) over the same period as in figure 1. Centini 
systems are charged for a 3 hour period programmed to be during a lower cost off -peak tariff window from 
00:00 to 07:00 each night, Whins systems are set to charge the hot water tanks to comfort temperature eac h 
morning prior to morning demand,  and provide daytime space heating  if required on a standard daily profile  
(am and pm 'occupied' heating periods at 21 oC with night and day setback at 18 oC). The Whins have a 
programmed steriliz e cycle which boost-heats th e tank to 70 oC periodically. The Centini and Whins average 
heating plus hot water daily demands were measured to be 14 and 17kWh respectively for February, and 
3kWh for each type for June. This gives a potentially shiftable daily energy load for the 14 Cen tini plus 24 
Whins dwelling s of 600kWh in February and 120kWh in June. Each Centini has a heating load of 4.2kW durin g 
charging and the Whins 3kW. Total cumulative power able to be shifted was around 130kW.

5.1  MO D EL  P R EDI C TI V E  CO NT RO L FO R SO L AR  TH ER M A L S Y ST E M S 
The Centini  system is illustrated in figure 8 . Optimal control of  the system must consider the solar thermal 
inputs as well as the other system and user  characteristics in quantifying opportunities for shifting  the 
electrical heater inputs normally set for the night time off peak period.   
The situation is variable in summer and winter. In winter solar gains and solar flow temperatures are less and 
heating demands greater, so the synchronization of the electrical demand to best match renewable generation 
within the comfort window is the  primary concern.  In summer the re is significant potential fo r high 
temperature solar energy inputs and the aim becomes one of  maximising solar input  and avoiding backup 
heating while also making sure that any electricity required is renewable.  
To predict the load shift vectors (see section 3) it is necessary to capture system characteristics in a model.  The 
ideal model is detailed enough to represent system behavior but able to be informed by relatively simple 
monitoring. The approach taken in the ORIGIN project was to initially use a superset  of sensors to gather data 
to inform the model based algorithms , including solar, space heating, and domestic hot water heat meters 
(figure 12) and 6 tank temperatures ; subsequently a simpler and lower cost reduced set was considered. A  6 
segment energy balance model of the tank adapted from that of Duffie and Beckman [18] was selected where 
each segment has an energy stor ed associated with its mean temperature. These segment temperatures are 
then modified in the model based on relevant energy and water inputs and outputs. The 6 seg ment 
temperatures can be read for each timestep  from installed ORIGIN sensors , and then future temperatures 
predicted using the simple model.  
At each future timestep the predicted tank node temperatures are calculated using  the following sequence: 1. 
The temperature shifts due to predicted standing energy losses and internal circulations are applied (LOSS) ; 2. 
The temperature shifts due to the predicted hot water and space heating energy demands are applied 
(DHWout, SHout); 3. The calculated solar energy inputs (SOL in) are applied to nodes with temperatures below the 
predicted solar flow temperature (T sol); 4. If the electric heating system  is on , then the calculated heating 
energy inputs are applied to raise node temperature s towards their setpoints within the constraints of the 
power available (E in). Then applying the same 4 steps will predict the temperatures at the next timestep 30 
minutes after that, and so on, to give the predicted tank profile and energy inputs, loads a nd losses at each 
30min step for the future 48 hour period. These measured and predicted temperatures are generated for a

The underlying heat loss and de -stratification characteristics of the tank are determined as a function of 
segment temperature by regression analysis in periods without other energy inflows and outflows. Standard 
heating times and setpoints are  either inferred from the learning dataset , or from audit, or user inputs. The 
desired comfort temperatures are either set at standard values or can be inferred , or adjusted based on user 
inputs. Domestic hot water demands are set based on the pattern established from  the learning dataset from 
patterns on occupied days and may be modified based on user inputs, process control statistics are established 
(mean, sigma) to describe typical behaviour and may be used to detect and  respond to changes e.g. trigger a 
query to the occupant [19]. The level of service can be adjusted by altering the statistical safeguard , (nominally 


+1 daily sigma), used in demand forecasts. Whe n space heating demands are also met from the storage tank 
then demand (mean, sigma) is established from the rolling 5 week learning dataset as a function of time,  
outdoor temperature and solar radia tion. Solar input  characteristics are established as functions of tank 
conditions and solar illuminance from the learning dataset in a three step process: first the threshold 
illuminance below which no solar input occurs is established; then the solar flow temperature dependence on  
solar illuminance, tank temperatures, and outdoor temperature determined; finally the solar energy inpu t is 
determined as a function of the predicted solar flow temperature and tank segment temperatures for the 
given timestep.  
The model based prediction methodology has so far yielded promising results meeting required standards for 
building model calibration [20]. Figure 14 shows an example of predicted and actual performance of the model

important for the delivery of service. The largest discrepancy seen is for the lower temperature nodes due to 
the stochastic occupant wat er draws being different to the  smoother predicted profile, the opportunity 
quantification algorithm corrects for these offsets by re-forecasting based on the measured tank temperatures 
at each future  time-step. The mean pl us 1 sigma (conservative worst case) demands are assumed in the 
forward projections of the service window wit hin which the load can be flexed to provide a safeguard , this 
conservatism is shown in the lower top temperatures and higher predicted than actual loads in figure 14. The 
models are  iterated with the  mean water draw profile for load prediction . The  projection is updated each 
timestep based on measured tank temperatures so that stochastic behaviour is accounted for.

5.2  MO D ELI NG  ORIGIN  R EM O T E CO NTRO L  O F  EL E CT RI C AL  H EA T LO A DS. 
The pilot studies  in the project , while demonstrating significant potentials and capabilities, due to short 
timeframe and stochastic nature of weather , did not allow for more general quantifications or conclusions to 
be made  on the impact s of these load shifting methods if more  fully deployed  and over longer periods . 
Consequently a modeling exercise was carried  out to provide more insight into the possible impact of the 
system deployment.  
From a review  of software [21] with capability for mode ling renewable energy networks with load shifting  
HOMER was selected  [22, 23] due to its capability for representing load shi fting as deferrable loads . The 
'deferrable loads' function was used to capture characteristics of the 14 Centini and 24 Whins space heating 
and hot water systems (i.e. 600kWh and 120kWh per day shiftable load in Feb and June respectively as 
previously described in sections 2.1 and 5) . These were input to  a model of the existing Findhorn Community 


calibrated against monitoring data . The model was then run to capture t he potential increase in self-use of 
local renewable electricity generation due to shifting of these loads.  
 
Figure 19. Primary demand (red), Renewable generation (green), and shifted thermal demand (purple) (y axis 0 
to 800kWh/30min),  for a typical winter week (1-7 Jan) with Centini and Whins heating under ORIGIN control.  
Figure 19 illustrates the operation for a winter week under ORIGIN control  of the Centini and Whin heating 
loads. The bott om dark line shows the heating load now largely synchronized to  periods with renewable 
surplus. There are still several times when this l oad has to be supplied by non renewables but the majority is 
shifted to renewable generation. The ORIGIN remote control orchestration applied to  all 14 Centini and 25 
Whins space heating and hot wat er loads was projected to reduce grid imports by 65MWh from 511 to 
446MWh p.a. (13% reduction) and the corresponding increase in local renewable self-consumption was from 
621 to 686MWh (10% increase). 
While the HOMER modeling may be considered state-of-the-art it does not allow the detailed operation of the 
ORIGIN system to be fully comp rehended. These modeling results should be viewed as indicative rather than 
an accurate representation of the potential of the ORIGIN  orchestration system when applied to a Community 
with Findhorns characteristics. More work needs to be done to enable modeling of such systems.    
7. DISCUSSION . 
The informational plus tariff stimulus then, if the same system was implemented across the Findhorn 
community with the same response (21% engagement, average response), would decreased gri d imports from 
the current 511MWh p.a. to 505MWh (1.2% reduction). If all participants exhibited the ‘motivated participant’ 
response then imports would reduce by 38MWh to 573MWh (7.6% reduction). This 'motivated participant' 
potential would be unlikely to  be achieved through behavioral response since even if motivated not all 
occupants are able to respond at this level. On the other hand even the 'motivated participant' may have been 
able to achieve an even greater response if some automation had been avai lable to assist during times of 
sleeping or absence etc. In combination , the behavioral response plus the remote control of existing Centini 
and Whins heating systems could be projected to give 71MWh reduction in grid imports i.e. from 511 to 
440MWh, a 14% reduction in grid imports and corresponding 11% increase in self -consumption of local 
renewables). The predictive model based control of the solar thermal system provided very positive results in 
summer with electrical back up heating reduced by 44% and i ncrease in solar fraction. The alignment of 
backup electricity with local community renewable generation was found to be increased by 22% during the 
trial period. 


The remote control method for the solar thermal systems was informed by three installed heat meters on 
solar, hot water, and space heating circuits. In future it is proposed that these meters could be replaced: the 
solar with flow and return temperature measurements and  a solar pump power measurement; the hot water 
and space heat draws could be de termined from changes in temperature of the tank nodes; these lower cost 
approaches are the focus of ongoing work.  
The load shift algorithms presented here are for the solar thermal fed space and hot water heating systems 
present in Findhorn, similar but simplified algorithms were also deployed for appliance load shifting, vehicle 
charging and water pumping  although these loads were small compared to the thermal load . The algorithm 
presented here for solar thermal and community level wind renewable optimization can also be adapted for 
buildings with roof  mounted solar PV  through setting the objective function  to both maximize self -
consumption of the buildings local PV generation , and then align any remaining demands with community 
renewable surplus and/or low cost electricity.  
The integration of controls and a lgorithms would ideally be done by or in cooperation with system 
manufacturers and integrated with standard system controls. The implementation into existing systems as 
demonstrated in ORIGIN without such cooperation is of course possible. The controls and  algorithms can be 
deployed to communicate within a community ORIGIN system as demonstrated here, or be deployed as local 
single building controllers which act independently and optimize individual system operation based on tariff 
and weather data from the  internet or other source (through an ORIGIN or alternative web service). It is 
possible to implement safe default conditions for cases when internet updates are unavailable.       
The results reported here are of course specific to the Findhorn Community in its current configuration with 
only monitoring and control interventions. It would be possible to achieve much higher levels of demand 
response and reductions in energy imports if additional thermal and electrical storage was implemented .  
CONCLUSIONS  
The ORIGIN demand response system was implemented in the existing 75 building Findhorn Community which 
includes high levels of renewable generation , including wind and solar , and has low energy buildings and 
energy systems that may become more widespread in future. 
Informational feedback mechanisms were provided giving forecasts of periods of green energy surplus or 
green energy shortfall with the intent of stimulating behavioral change and reducing the use of grid energy. 
These feedback mechanisms included web and phone enabled widgets. The informational feedbacks on their 
own did not result in a sustained measurable increase in consumption of locally generated energy.   
A tariff system was deployed to give forecasts of energy price variations associated wit h renewable surplus 
and shortfall with a price ratio of 1:4. There was an initial observable response in 47% of participating 
households but only 21% were observed to be responsive throughout the full period of the variable tariff 
structure. Some very responsive ‘motivated participants’ could be identified who strongly engaged.  
Across the Community it was projected that a 1.2% reduction in grid imports could be achieved if the same 
21% response rate and average overall response seen on the trial was replicated, if the ‘motivated participant’ 
behavior could be replicated across the Community then there could be a 7.6% reduction , however this is 
unlikely in the wider population. 
A remote control algorithm was implemented on solar f ed space heating and hot water systems and shown to 
reduce backup electric heating energy us e by 44% in summer and increase  alignment of backup heating with 
renewable generation by 22%. 

The implementation of the remote control scheme across the 38 electric ally heated dwellings with suitable 
systems was projected to reduce annual  grid imports by 13% with a corresponding increase in self -
consumption of renewable generation. 
These result s were for the specifics of  the Findhorn situation with only the ORIGIN system implemented . It 
would be possible to design future changes in infrastructure to enhance the effectiveness of the ORIGIN 
system and further reduce grid imports.  Some preliminary modeling studies were carried out but more work is 
required to increase the level of certainty in their predictive skill.
